And they are the realities of the beginnings of the worlds of invention: they are 24 worlds, and in each world is the reality of one luminous letter. That single letter thus governs the manifestation of each of these worlds. Since the reality of the upper and lower world is a relation within the very being of the Throne, the secret of their stability within it lies in the two lines written in light, by which I mean white light and green light. They are: “There is no god but God; Muhammad is the Messenger of God.” Those two luminous lines are beneath the Throne, so understand the reality of this luminous subtlety. Since the spirits of the Malakūt realities and the lights of the Jabarūt realities issue from the eight who bear the Throne, the whole upper world is the lights of lights, and it is the Throne; the illuminator of the light is God. Each angel has 3 letters. From each light a slanting letter emerges; then the horizon of every Malakūt and Jabarūt and celestial sphere [UNCLEAR: the meaning of this fragment after “slanting” is uncertain]; the light of Malakūt, with its ink, is speech [UNCLEAR: the meaning of this clause is uncertain]. The light of Jabarūt is with the spirits, and the light of Mulk sustains the heart. Thus 24 are completed for the eight angels, from multiplying three by eight. Therefore, for whoever says, “There is no god but God; Muhammad is the Messenger of God,” the utterance reaches the Throne. This is because the good word ascends of itself: it is at ease in Mulk, an ascension in Jabarūt, and a rising in Malakūt. God Most High says: “To Him good words ascend, and righteous work raises it.” Therefore, according to the text, whoever says it at bedtime with the count kūr [UNCLEAR: the meaning and numerical value of this count are uncertain], his spirit appears beneath the Throne, nourished from that according to its powers.
