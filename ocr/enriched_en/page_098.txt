( Section ) As for his saying, "and four hā have been ḍufifat for our fighting" [UNCLEAR: whether hā is a letter-name or an incomplete expression, and the reading and meaning of ḍufifat, are uncertain], he means the extracted form of these four from al-jadūshaq, musammara ("nailed") [UNCLEAR: al-jadūshaq is opaque, and the connection of "nailed" is uncertain]. The text claims: whoever breaks them up, places them on an iron sheet, makes their number into a broken-up wafq in the [body] of the sheet, and carries it with him on his head, on the outside of the cap, and meets the enemy in war, will never suffer any harm, even if he throws himself among spearheads, sword-blades, and other such things; he will be victorious over his enemy.
( Section ) As for his saying, "and the moon in ṭurūf khushūsh, darrat" [UNCLEAR: the reading, meaning, and syntax of this phrase are uncertain], and it is worked into a cap, and afterward it is fumigated with whatever is available of the four scents. Then recite over it the Name that is in Sūrat al-Aʿrāf, namely the saying of God Most High: "Indeed, your Lord is God, who created the heavens and the earth in six days." And he puts it on; the text claims that he is then safe in places of fear and in the presence of wrongdoers and people who cause harm.

( Section ) As for his saying, * "and a seal for good, exalted are its attributes" * he means the last seal, namely the split hā. When it is written with the wāw repeated after it, then, if you wish to have needs fulfilled, the text claims that it is useful. For nullifying magic, untying what is knotted, making matters easy, the delivery of pregnant women, binding the adversary's tongue, freeing a captive, releasing an imprisoned person, seeking provision, increasing the blessing in food, and extinguishing a man's rage, we write, and it is carried in the name of whomever you intend; the text claims that you will see wonders from its blessing. * If it is written in reverse, that is, with the wāw before the hā and repeated, and after it five split hās, the text claims that it clears away worries, thoughts, obsessive whisperings, frightening dreams, and bleeding from the body's openings. It is also written for the disabling of al-māsh, the shop, the woman hindered from marriage, and movement from travel, in barkān or in a sea [UNCLEAR: al-māsh and barkān are opaque; the connections of the woman, movement, travel, and the final locations are grammatically uncertain]. After fumigating the sheet with myrrh, aloes, and asafoetida, put it in a Persian reed, wind a red silk thread around it, tie the thread to a hunter's lead shot, and bury it in a water channel flowing east. The text claims that the person for whom the working is done will not cease bleeding from his openings until he [perishes] for you [UNCLEAR: the connection of "for you" is uncertain]. It belongs to the worlds of Mars; it is worked for the shedding of blood and the perishing of souls, as a tremendous working.
