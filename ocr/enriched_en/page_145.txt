English:
— 119 —

…for a number of years I accompanied him and his brothers in something of that. When the companionship between me and him lengthened, and one day we began to converse about the topic of the spiritual exercise (riyāḍah) and what some of the awliyā', may Allah be pleased with them, mentioned concerning its conduct — that the root of all matters is the piety (taqwā) of Allah, exalted, the purity of intention, sincerity (ikhlāṣ), and the seeking of the Hereafter and the highest degree with those whom Allah has favoured: the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous, and excellent are those as companions.

So the shaykh said to me of his own accord: “By Allah, O my brother 'Abd al-Wāḥid, I am not in blessing and good except by the spiritual exercise of Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ.” So I smiled, and he said to me: “Why did you smile? Do you mock what I told you?” I said: “Far is it from Allah, exalted, that I mock the attribute of the Most Merciful, the Lord of might and greatness.” But the reason for my smile was that, by Allah, I did not intend you from the lands of the Maghrib and my love for you except for it. He said: “You say thus by the right of the Lord of this House?” I said: “Yes.” He said to me: “The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, said: ‘Whoever intends us and loves the right due to us, and I follow his word and hold to his law — when he intended me, his right upon me became due; and you are from the people of knowledge and intended me from distant lands and have beautiful patience, for you had a period and did not know me.’ As for the reason for your love of me, it indicates your abundant intellect and good knowledge. By the Lord of this House, I will not withhold it from you.”

So I kissed his hand and my head, and he said: “Tomorrow, if Allah, exalted, wills, I will present it to you and make known to you its method.” So I prayed for him for a good end, and I slept that night from joy. When I went early to the Noble Ka'bah and circumambulated at dawn, behold, the shaykh was sitting in his place of yesterday; so I came to him and kissed his hand. He said: “Do you know what I say to you and what I point out to you?” I said: “No, by Allah.” He said: “By Allah, my shaykh 'Abd al-Ṣamad al-Khwārazmī taught me names which I would recite at the time of sleep ten times, and send blessings upon the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, and ask Allah, exalted, to unveil what I desire, and it is unveiled to me by the blessing of these names. And if you do that this night and ask Allah, exalted, concerning your affair, and your intention in this spiritual exercise was a worldly or otherworldly need — O people of ‘I do’ or ‘I do not do’ from my greatness — then it was as if I saw my shaykh 'Abd al-Ṣamad al-Khwārazmī in a dream, and he said: ‘O Abā 'Abd Allāh, you asked about the state of 'Abd al-Wāḥid, or he intended you because of this noble spiritual exercise, so do not send him back disappointed from it, for he is from the people of good and worthy of it; but covenant with him to preserve it and conceal it from other than its people, and make known to him that whenever he changes his intention, grave harm befalls him from its servants’ — and we ask Allah, exalted, for well-being.” And he said to me: “Convey my greetings to him.” 'Abd al-Wāḥid said: So I wept greatly and prostrated in thanks to Allah, exalted; then he covenanted with me at the Black Stone that I would convey this great secret only to its people, and enjoined me to the piety of Allah, exalted. Then he handed me a folio in which the description of this noble spiritual exercise was written, and it was mentioned in it that whoever desires the spiritual exercise of Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ must be sincere, and he purifies himself, cleans himself, and bathes, and sits in a place empty of people where he speaks to none but Allah, exalted, during the mentioned period — but the one who serves him is a righteous man, sincere in service, eager for purity and cleanliness. And you fast for the spiritual exercise from the first Thursday of the month, completing fifteen days fasting from all that has a soul, but his breaking of the fast is upon barley bread, salt, and oil; and he recites the noble sūrah five thousand times each day — a thousand times after each obligatory prayer, and a thousand times at midnight — for a period of fourteen days, so its total is eighty-four thousand times. And the rest of its times are with whatever is easy of recitation, remembrance, and blessings upon the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, and the incense is a work by night and day — its description is nadd and ḥaṣā lubān and Jāwī.
