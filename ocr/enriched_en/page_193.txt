( The Sixty-Seventh Chapter: On His name, exalted, { al-Wāḥid } ) This ṣamadānī name and spiritual secret — whoever abounds in its remembrance becomes averse to multiplicity; and in it is a subtle secret for one who wants to make a man or woman barren of children — let him abound in its remembrance with that intention and it is obtained for him; so let him fear Allah, exalted. And it is of the invocations of the great ones; and the author of Taysīr al-Maṭālib, may Allah sanctify his spirit, said: "This name is nearest of names to the Essence; and when added to the comprehensive name it was of the greatest and most majestic invocations"; and he alluded to a subtlety for one whose name is Aḥmad. And know that His name al-Wāḥid and al-Aḥad are a majestic remembrance of great standing for the wayfarers attached to the secrets of monotheism; and Abū 'Abd Allāh al-Kūfī said: "His name al-Aḥad fits the people of annihilation in the presence of gathering, for they witness none but One"; and whoever abounds in its remembrance — Allah, exalted, opens upon him monotheism; and this is its form as you see [an 8×8 square of the letters of Aḥad/Wāḥid]:

And whoever engraves these two noble names on paper in the first [hour] of Sunday while facing the qibla in purity and remembrance and places them at his head — Allah, exalted, provides him glory, awe, dignity, and greatness; and this name has the number 13, which is the number of Wālī (Governor), for the meaning of al-Aḥad is a seamlessness with no split in it; and as for the names of its letters, they are 65: the first number points to His name, exalted, Matīn (Firm) for what is in oneness of the meaning of the name of Allah, exalted — therefore it came after it in Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ; and it also points to His name, exalted, 'Alī (High) for what is in loftiness of the perceptions of creation for it being an exposition of the comprehensive name; and the second number points to His name Mu'annis (Consoling) — for by the oneness of the Real every lonely one is consoled from other than Him; and whoever abounds in its remembrance becomes averse to multiplicity; and this is the form of its square as you see:

Abū 'Abd Allāh al-Kūfī, may Allah sanctify his secret, said in his book Kanz al-Asrār: Whoever places these greatly-ranked names — which are: Allah, Aḥad, Wāḥid, Jawād (Generous), Wahhāb (Bestower), Ḥayy (Living), Mūjid (Originating), Dā'im (Everlasting), Walī (Friend), Mujīb (Responder), Wadūd (Loving), Awwal (First), Hādī (Guide) — in a square and deposits it in the interior of the square of Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ and carries it with him, witnesses of the wonders of the making of Allah, exalted, what cannot be encompassed — for every one of these names gives its bearer what is in its power of the life of the heart, the spirit of gnoses, and the refinements of monotheism; and if a possessor of a true state persists in its remembrance, Allah widens for him his inward and outward provision, and he asks Allah, exalted, for nothing but He gives it to him; and it is of the greatest invocations, and its end is the goal; and it is placed for kings.
