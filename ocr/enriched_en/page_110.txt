The decrees of the Sole King in His Throne — know that the Throne displays within itself the traces of Power from the All-Powerful. Therefore the shīn stands at the end of the letters of the Throne, and it is of the oneness of the many worlds. Since the ordained ordering and the arraying of every thing was made a throne, the shīn became the throne of the letters — and that because of the loftiness of its rank and its ordering. And there is not found among the letters that which completes their thrones, [apart from] the letter alif, because alif is the root of the tree of the letters, and the shīn is for it the end of the letters and their rising; and there is no branch after it except one from its interior. Therefore the alif is not preceded by anything except that which is of it, since the shīn is like the shape of the alif spread out from three letters — thus sh, y, n — and its proportion is the same as its proportion. And if it were otherwise than the shīn, composed of three letters, it would not be a throne for the shīn, because it does not terminate in an end, on account of its resemblance and its firmness.

Likewise it was set forth earlier from His words “God bears witness,” as an indication of the firmness of the tawḥīd and of the non-existence [of anything else] in the two abodes and the two realms; and the sīn is al-Kursī. Nor is it far-fetched that al-Kursī is that which bears it — that is, the Throne — since you see that the body is a throne for the throne of the sun. In truth, every subtle thing subsists by means of every dense thing. Therefore the alif was the lightest and most subtle of the letters, because it has no likeness, and its standing establishes a standing one. And it has no like among the single letter-units, and none of the others is known to be over it, nor does another of them precede it at the end of the word. So it points to origin and to end — except that al-Kursī is more fitting as an attribute of the world of the Throne.

And since the shīn was the last in the rank of the Throne in the general, its last in detail is the nūn; and the nūn is the bearer of the cosmoses, that is, the whale. So the nūn is derived from the shīn, and the cosmoses are derived from [the nūn]; and likewise the lofty world is derived from the nūn. God Most High said: “Nūn, and the pen and what they write.” So the pen is derived from that nūn, which is the outer [aspect] of that command whose inner is the kāf, indicating the hidden secret. And this is the shīn: it is not made to be inscribed. And the letter shīn is written in it a thousand times in the first hour of each day that befits its deed, because among the days there is that which is sought for good, and among them there is that which is sought for evil, such as Saturday and its hour; and its hour, for every secret that befits its deed; and in it there is that which [UNCLEAR: corrupt words here] befalls it and its deed — God makes easy for it what it seeks, and whatever its aim, of good or of evil.

And the secrets of the shīn in the bodily world are more than can be counted, except that it does not bear from it an ache in its limbs, because that pain is too strong for the one for whom it is written, except a woman in labour, for whom childbirth is made light by her distress. And in it there is harm beyond counting, and this limb has fallen into His name al-Shadīd, the Severe.

And whoever knows the joining-point (raqbah) of the shīn and where its relation to nature stands — in general, which is the shīn, and in particular, which is sh, y, n — and what belongs to it of natures and of numerical correspondence, knows its secrets; and whoever knows what belongs to it of inflections and of inward states, then the ‘ayn is derived from al-‘Alā’, above whom there is nothing and above whom there is no exaltation; and the sīn is derived from the raḥmah, above which there is no raḥmah and below whose light no one is given raḥmah; and the shīn is derived from the shahādah, above which there is no shahādah and below which there is no witness. So consider how you find the shahādah to be both witnessed and witnessing, and the raḥmah to be both the one given raḥmah and the giver of raḥmah. And you will find for al-‘Alā’ no exaltation and no being exalted over, except the worshipped Lordship, conditioned on the necessary obedience — the glory of His Messenger and of the believers. The divine glory is the everlasting duration and the pre-eternity; the glory of the prophets is the existence of the message; and the glory of the believers is the existence of faith [in God]. And these are the three ranks of the shīn in al-Shahīd, the Witness.
