[Continuation concerning al-Iklīl]

[UNCLEAR: al-Iklīl is described as a crown upon the scorpion's head.]

As for al-Qalb, it is the scorpion's heart. Beside it is a luminous star, and beside that star are two stars. Its form is as follows: 0 0 0.

As for al-Shawlah, they are two separated stars called the Scorpion's star. Their form is as follows: 0 0. Some say that it is the scorpion's tail, derived from al-shawl, meaning elevation, as though it were raised, prominent, and projecting. Some say that it lies outside the Milky Way. It has also been said that it resembles the timbers suspended over the mouth of a well, into which the pulley and ropes are placed; they are called al-Naʿāʾim. ☼

As for al-Baldah, it is six stars of Sagittarius, and the Sun reaches it on the shortest day of the year. Some say that al-Baldah is the opening between the two [UNCLEAR: special points], and its form is as follows: 0 0. ☼

As for al-Dhābiḥ, they are two stars with a distance of one cubit between them. Each has a small star close to it, as though it were slaughtering that star; thus it was called al-Dhābiḥ. Its form is [FORM NOT SPECIFIED IN OCR]. ☼

As for Saʿd al-Suʿūd, they are two stars, one of them luminous, with a fourth star below one of them. Its form is [FORM NOT SPECIFIED IN OCR]: 0 0 0 0 0 0 0 0 0. As for Saʿd al-Akhbiyah, they are three, as though they were one star. Its form is as follows: 0 0. ☼

It is said that they are two stars. Some say that the first is Saʿd al-Suʿūd, followed by Saʿd al-Dhābiḥ, then Saʿd al-Akhbiyah, then Saʿd Bulaʿ; these are four, and the Moon enters them once a year. The other lunar mansions are Saʿd Qāʿirah, Saʿd al-Mulk, Saʿd Hammām, Saʿd Bāriʿ, and Saʿd Naẓīr. Each Saʿd among these six consists of two stars; to the eye, the distance between each pair is one cubit, and they are the key to the year.

As for the two branches of al-Dālī, al-Muqaddam and al-Muʾakhkhar, each consists of two stars, with five cubits between one and the other to the eye. They appear as though they are pouring from buckets. Al-Fargh is the outlet of water from the bucket, and for this reason the two Farghs are so called. ☼

As for al-Rashā, it is a small star that the Moon enters. These are the lunar mansions through which it travels each month. Every night the Moon is in one part of them: fourteen between sunrise and sunset, and fourteen between sunset and sunrise. At dawn it enters both. As it rises from east to west, their rising is to the left of the person praying and their setting is to his right, according to the differences among the climates. The Sun also enters these mansions.

Know that the Arabs call the mansions al-Anwāʾ. They were given this name because when the setting star falls, the rising star rises. It is also said that al-Nawʾ is the setting of a star from the mansions in the west together with the rising of its counterpart opposite it in the east, for one hour each night except thirteen; and this applies to each star until the end of the year, except al-Jabhah, which has fourteen days. ☼

I say: We have not heard al-Nawʾ described as “falling” except in this passage. The Arabs attributed rains, winds, cold, and movement to it. ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, may Allah be pleased with him, said: “We were rained upon by such-and-such a nawʾ.”

[Section on the rules governing their risings]

The rising of al-Sharṭayn is on the thirteenth of Nīsān, and the Sun enters without al-Iklīl. The rising of al-Buṭayn is on the remaining night of that month. The rising of al-Thurayyā is on the thirteenth of Ayyār. After it sets at sunset, it remains hidden for fifty nights, then appears in the morning from the east. When it is midway in the sky as the Sun sets, the cold intensifies and afflictions are lifted from the fruits.
