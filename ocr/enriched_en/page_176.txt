( The Twenty-Sixth Chapter: On His name, exalted, { al-Mudhill } ) This name of noble standing — whoever abounds in its remembrance, Allah humbles to him whatever He wills of his enemies; and it is fitting that everyone whom a beast or one of creation is intractable toward should abound in its remembrance, for Allah, exalted, humbles it to him. And whoever takes it as a remembrance after fasting three days, the last of them Friday, and refrains from breaking the fast on Friday, prays two rak'ahs, and mentions the name a hundred times after al-Fātiḥa; and when he prostrates does likewise; and in the second rak'ah likewise; and gives salām and mentions the name a thousand times after the salām, and says "O Mudhill, humble to me so-and-so" — he is humbled to him and does not oppose him in any matter. ( A subtlety ) When the people of insight beheld with the fingers of their hearts that the place of humiliation is the Lām of gentleness and connection, they knew there is no connection to Him but by humiliation — so they equalled the dogs in eating from the dung-heaps, and the undying glory was completed for them; and they were delivered from the plight of humiliation to other than Him, until they knew with certainty and witnessed favour, and knew that there is no humbler but Allah.
