And whoever carries it is under God's protection. It affords great acceptance to one who enters the presence of
kings, sultans, and great men: God protects its bearer from them, and its bearer is supported and victorious,
overcoming everyone who opposes him. It is beneficial for nullifying magic, untying what is knotted, and helping
one whose imprisonment has lasted a long time; it also benefits a person suffering seizures and expels the
indwelling entity from the body. It is hung on the afflicted person, and if the indwelling entity remains, that
entity burns. Whoever engraves it on a silver ring in the first hour of Friday must have the engraver remain
silent. Wear it as a ring: no one sees the wearer without loving him and fulfilling his need. If the wearer
enters a sultan's presence, he attains his purpose, but he must put the ring on his right hand. * Whoever enters
battle is to have it on his left hand. * If the ring is placed in a ruined place, that place becomes populated
and built up. * If an unmarried woman carries it, she marries, especially a virgin. * If someone who fears
highwaymen and every other unwelcome thing carries it, he is safe from them. * If it is hung on an army's
banner, its army is victorious. A king from China had been besieging a city of the unbelievers for a long time
[UNCLEAR: the source also says bi-hi, 'with it'; its referent is uncertain], until the Muslims built another
city around that city, yet they could not conquer it. Some people told the king of a man known for asceticism,
piety, and learning, who had become famous for these qualities. Then the king [UNCLEAR: fa-khabbāhu, possibly
'hid him'; the reading and intended action are uncertain] and said to him, "Aid us with supplications." The king
told the man how long it had been and that they had been unable to conquer this city. The shaykh took a slip and
restored the Name on it, repeated and expanded [UNCLEAR: the source verb rammama means 'restored/repaired'; the
intended action on the slip is uncertain], and said to the king, "Put it at the front of your head and advance
against the unbelievers." The king did so; and, by God, an hour had not passed before God gave the Muslims
victory, they took possession of the city, and they gained booty. The king sent the shaykh a portion of the
booty, but the shaykh would not accept it, saying, "I have the greater booty." * Among its properties is the
following: a man of the family of Jaʿfar al-Manṣūr was sought by the king, then he sought him [UNCLEAR: the
repeated request, fa-ṭalabahu, does not clearly identify its subject or object]. When he came, his complexion
changed [UNCLEAR: whose arrival and whose complexion are meant is uncertain], and he handed him a slip
containing the Greatest Name [UNCLEAR: the giver and recipient are not clearly identified]. The caliph ordered
his beheading [UNCLEAR: the person intended is not explicitly identified]. The executioner's hands withdrew from
his sword. The caliph ordered the executioner a second and a third time, but the executioner still did not cut.
Then someone said to them, "Search him" [UNCLEAR: the linking words fa-lahu, 'so/to him,' and the identity of
the speaker are uncertain]. They found that slip, containing the wafq, and were astonished. Whoever God has
favored with this secret must guard it, for these seven letters were written on the door of the Kaʿbah, and they
operate in 72 workings for bringing forth things sought, buried valuables, and treasures. * If you wish to work
with them, write them in saffron and hang them on the neck of a rooster described as ahūshar [UNCLEAR: the
reading and meaning of this description are uncertain]. Release the rooster at the site of the buried valuables
or hidden cache. Wherever it stands and scratches with its foot or its beak, or crows, there are the buried
valuables or hidden cache. * If you wish to demolish fortresses, citadels, and places, and lay them waste,
impress the seal of the work of good in wax, and on the other side impress the seal of the work of evil. Then
bury it beneath the door's threshold and pour over it some of the bathhouse's escaping water [UNCLEAR: māʾ hārib
al-ḥammām, literally water escaping from the bathhouse; the precise kind of runoff is uncertain]. * If you wish
to expel someone from a town, take a small bird and tie the signet at its neck, together with the name of the
person for whom the working is done and his mother's name. Tie it to the bird's leg with a yellow thread, and
release the bird with your left hand from behind your own back. As you release it, say, "Fulān son of Fulānah
has fled this place, by the right of these Names." * If you wish to effect removal and transformation, write the

such that you see nothing but their dwellings"—the verse. And say, "Come, haste!" * If you wish to bring about
stoning, write it on an unfired potsherd and bury it in the uppermost part of his house. Write on it, "And We

evil: you will see a wonder. * If you wish for war and the casting of fire into the oppressor's house, take a
