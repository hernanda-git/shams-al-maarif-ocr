English:
— 27 —

Know—may God grant you and us success in obeying Him—that the Exalted has spoken about the twelve zodiac signs and the twenty-eight lunar mansions: “We have certainly placed constellations in the heaven and adorned it for the observers”; and the Exalted said: “Blessed is He who placed constellations in the heaven,” [the remainder of] the verse; and the Exalted said: “Possessor of the constellations”; and the Exalted said: “We determined it as mansions.” Al-burj is the singular of al-buruj, and a burj is a palace; it may also be called a fortress. The Exalted said: “Even if you were in fortified towers.” Some scholars said regarding the Exalted’s saying, “Blessed is He who placed constellations in the heaven”: They are the locations of the seven wandering planets, and they are twelve constellations. The first is al-Hamal (Aries), followed by al-Thawr (Taurus), al-Jawzaʾ (Gemini), al-Saraṭān (Cancer), al-Asad (Leo), al-Sunbulah (Virgo), al-Mīzān (Libra), al-ʿAqrab (Scorpio), al-Qaws (Sagittarius), al-Jady (Capricorn), al-Dalw (Aquarius), and al-Ḥūt (Pisces). Al-Hamal and al-ʿAqrab are the domiciles of Mars; al-Thawr is the domicile of Venus; al-Jawzaʾ and al-Sunbulah are the domiciles of Mercury; al-Saraṭān is the domicile of the Moon; al-Asad is the domicile of the Sun; al-Ḥūt is the domicile of Jupiter; and al-Jady and al-Dalw are the domiciles of Saturn. These constellations are divided among the four natures, with three constellations in each group, called triplicities. Al-Hamal, al-Asad, and al-Qaws form the fiery triplicity; al-Thawr, al-Sunbulah, and al-Jady the earthy triplicity; al-Jawzaʾ, al-Mīzān, and al-Dalw the airy triplicity; and al-Saraṭān, al-ʿAqrab, and al-Ḥūt the watery triplicity. The people of interpretation differed concerning the meaning of al-buruj. Some said that they are palaces in the heaven, citing His saying: “Even if you were in fortified towers.” Others said that they are stars; others said lamps; and others said the gates of heaven called the Milky Way. I say: Ibn ʿAbbās, may God be pleased with them both, explicitly stated that they are the familiar constellations to which we have referred—twelve constellations—and that God, exalted is He, divided them into quadrants and triads. They are divided among the seven planets, as we mentioned on page 32. Among its stars are al-Thurayyā and al-Dabarān, which are lunar mansions.

The third is al-Jawzaʾ (Gemini), known as the Twins. It has eighteen stars, seven outside the figure. Its figure resembles two standing twins: one has placed his hand on the other’s shoulder; the head and the rest of its stars are in the north, its legs at the eastern edge, and their feet toward the west.

The fourth is al-Saraṭān (Cancer), which has six stars, with four outside its figure. It resembles a crab, with its front toward the east and its rear toward the west. The south is behind the Twins.
