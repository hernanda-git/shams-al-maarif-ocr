When the Pleiades appear in the evening, the small-eyed one sees them as seven stars. § They follow the liver of al-Ḥarrāʾ, as though they were a splint in a knee above a wrist.

The form of al-Thurayyā is as follows: 0 0 0. § Al-Kaff al-Khaḍīb is al-Thurayyā spread out, and it has another part called al-Jazmā, which is below al-Sharṭayn. Al-ʿAyyūq is a large red star, and three stars follow behind it; among them is one called al-Aʿlām. They are followers of the lunar mansions, and we mention them here only because of their proximity to al-Thurayyā. §

As for al-Dabarān, it is the rump of Aries. It was called by this name, it is said, because it turned its back to al-Thurayyā. Some say that it is five stars in Taurus called Shāmah, and its form is as follows: 0 0. § It is also said to be a red star, and its form is as follows: 0. § Al-Dabarān is also called al-ʿAtīq because it resembles a great mountain. In front of it are small stars called al-Qalāṣ, meaning the young she-camels. When they gather, they appear in the form of a cow's head, and al-Dabarān follows al-Thurayyā. §

As for al-Haqʿah, it is three stars, some close to the others, and it is the head of al-Jawzāʾ, as though they were three joined fingers. Its form is as follows: 0 0 0. § It is also said to be the circle located on the side of a mount by the rider's leg. § Ibn ʿAbbās, may Allah be pleased with them both, was asked about a man who divorced his wife by the number of the stars in the sky. He said, “The al-Haqʿah of al-Jawzāʾ is sufficient for him.” §

As for al-Hanʿah, it is five separated stars: two large stars with three small ones between them. Apparently, they are five, and their form is as follows: 0 0 0 0 0. § Al-Hanʿah was given this name because each one inclines toward its companion. It is said: “haʿat the thing” when you bend it. §

As for al-Dhirāʿ, it is the arm, the arm of the lion, and it consists of two luminous stars. It is also said that they are two stars with small stars between them, as though they were the lion's claws. To the eye, the distance between them is the length of a whip. They are two arms, one extended and the other clenched; they are not aligned with the arm, and the extended one is higher than al-Simāk.

The larger of them is called al-Shiʿrā al-Ghamīṣāʾ because it was unable to cross the Milky Way, its companion caught up with it, and it wept until it became dim-eyed. It is also said that it wept because it did not catch up with Suhayl. §

As for al-Nathrah, there is a small distance between them containing a patch of whiteness, as though it were pieces of cloud. It is the lion's nose. It is also said to be three stars, and its form is as follows: 0 0 0. § It lies between the lion's mouth and nostril and is called Maḥaṭṭat al-Asad, “the lion's station.” §

As for al-Ṭarfah, they are two stars in front of the forehead, and they are the lion's eye. It consists of four stars, one of them a bright star, the Yamāniyy. As for al-Simāk, they are two luminous stars: al-Aʿzal, which is one of the lunar mansions, and al-Simāk al-Rāmih, which is not one of the lunar mansions. It is said that they are the lion's two legs, and it is also said that they are the lion's two shanks, each being the other shank. With al-Rāmih there is a star in front of it, which is its spear. Al-Aʿzal is isolated from the stars, with no star near it. It was called al-Aʿzal because it has no spear, and it was called Simāk because it rose high in the sky.
