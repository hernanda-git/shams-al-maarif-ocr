English:

Among them is what appears together with them, and they are fourteen letters, as you see: alif, bāʾ, jīm, ḥāʾ, kāf, mīm, ʿayn, ghayn, fāʾ, qāf, hāʾ, wāw, and yāʾ. The first of the letters is alif, followed by letters such as acts of obedience, definiteness forms, and rāʾs, all belonging to the side of alif and what follows it. When an observer considers the letters, he finds them corresponding within the soul before they exist in physical form, so understand. Alif among the letters is the one among the numbers, and numbers are a subtle spiritual power. Reliance upon it is therefore among the secrets of utterances, just as letters are among the secrets of actions. Numbers in the human world have secrets and benefits that He, exalted is His power, has arranged just as letters are arranged: secrets of benefit such as supplication, incantation, and whatever else whose effect becomes manifest to the world through various names. (And know)
that letters have no time that confines them; rather, they act through spiritual discipline and special properties for whomever He wills, while numbers act through talismans and are connected to higher-world correspondences. (As for the letter dāl,) it has the numerical value four. Whoever constructs a figure should multiply 4 by 4 and place within it the correspondence of Monday—the day the Prophet was born, the day he was commissioned, and the day he died—under the moon's exaltation at three degrees of Taurus, free from malefic influences. Let the hour belong to the moon, and write it after complete purification and two cycles of prayer, reciting Āyat al-Kursī and al-Ikhlāṣ one hundred times, on pure parchment. Whoever carries this wafq with him will have memorization and understanding made easy for him by God, and his rank will be magnified before the upper and lower worlds alike. If a prisoner carries it, he will be freed by God's permission. Whoever carries it on a banner will defeat the enemies among the unbelievers and aggressors. Whoever carries it and disputes with someone through it will overcome and subdue him. Its numerical arrangement is 4 by 4, and its form is made by multiplying 4 by 4. It belongs to the four elements—fire, air, earth, and water—and to the four humors: yellow bile, phlegm, blood, and black bile. These are four, not a quarter; thus it has the power of the temperaments and their balance. This noble letter appears especially in His name al-Dāʾim, and in His name al-Wadūd. Nothing precedes dāl in al-Dāʾim, and for that reason the two noble and generous names are Aḥmad and Muḥammad. This indicates that permanence is the final end of its beginning: it follows dāl in the word for permanence. It was placed first in His name al-Dāʾim because He possesses permanence at both beginning and end, and He granted His servants a share in enduring existence after annihilation in the Hereafter. This letter belongs to the Throne because its existence does not change: it is the first world of originated things, the first world, and the place to which spirits ascend. In it are the degrees of intellects and the lights of mercy. Some people who know God have disclosed this in connection with the portion allotted to Ḥārithah, may God be pleased with him, when the Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him, “How did you awake, O Ḥārithah?”
Ḥārithah replied, “O Messenger of God, I awoke a true believer.” The Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, asked, “What is the reality of your faith?”
Ḥārithah replied, “I awoke having presented myself to the world; its stones and gold, its living and dead, its rich and poor, had become equal to me. It was as though I were looking upon the Throne of the Merciful unveiled, while people were being driven to the reckoning, to Paradise, and to the Fire.” The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him, “You have understood, so remain steadfast.” The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, also said concerning spirits: “When they appear in a state of purity from ablution, they remain prostrating beneath the Throne.” (The letter dāl) has among its secrets permanence and abiding existence. (As for al-Wadūd,) it is a name derived from wudd. Wudd is a shared term: it is the outward aspect of love, while love is the inward aspect of wudd. The beginning of wudd is affection. Wudd is divided into outward and inward: its outward is wudd and its inward is love. Wudd dwells in the heart and is the heart's most revealing world. Al-shaqq is a subtlety between love and wudd; its dwelling is shaghaf, and the most beloved inward aspect of passion dwells in the fuʾād, because the heart has three cavities.
