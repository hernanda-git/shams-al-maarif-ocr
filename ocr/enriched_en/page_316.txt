And it has one name by which it is invoked: "O Allah, indeed I ask You — O Pure — by what You lodged in the letter Zāy of the stored secrets — that You subject to me the servants of these names. Indeed You are over all things Able." ( The Letter Sīn ) It is a speaking letter, hot and moist, earthy-air, its moisture moderate. And whoever breaks its letter-compound in a 4-by-4 square, and a woman looks at it while she is divorcing — she gives birth at once. And whoever writes it in Arabic 3 in a triangle in a copper vessel, and wipes it with fresh water and olive oil, and gives the stung one to drink from it — he is cured at once. And whoever places the numbers of its letter-compound in an open (space) on Friday first hour, upon a crystal ring-stone, and seals with it — provision is made easy for him, every difficulty is made easy for him, and he is safe from every disliked thing as long as it is in his hand. And whoever places the Arabic form of Sīn upon a clay seal, sows wheat, and hangs it in a place — the wolves do not approach it. And whoever writes an Indian Sīn in a mirror within a circle, and the one with a stitch-pain looks in it — he sees. And it has names by which its state is invoked: "O Allah, indeed I ask You — O All-Hearing, O All-Hearing [S S S S] — by what You lodged in the letter Sīn of the stored secrets — that You subject to me Your noble angels [S S S S]. Indeed You are over all things Able." ( The Letter Shīn ) It is a speaking letter, hot and moist [Sh Sh Sh Sh], dry among the Greeks and Indians, and among the people of the West cold and moist; and it is the last rank of the cyclones, and its degree is moderate in heat. And it has properties of squared effect. And whoever writes it 13 times upon a sheet on Sunday with the Sun in Aries, and mounts it upon an amber seal, and carries it upon his head — Allah clothes him in a garment of awe and splendor from His extension, none sees him but loves him and enters under his obedience. And whoever breaks its letter-compound on Friday in the seventh hour upon copper gilded with gold, and carries it with him — men and jinn love him. And whoever mixes the names of persons with this letter upon a copper sheet, and places it near fire — that person or persons wander toward the place. And whoever breaks its spelling in a triangle upon red silk, and fumes it with frankincense, and writes around it His saying, exalted: { That they should not prostrate to Allah who brings forth what is hidden in the heavens } (the verse), and hangs it upon the neck of a white rooster on Sunday, and releases it in the place where the buried treasure or magic is — it stops upon it, crows, and scratches with its foot or beak 3 times, and crows 3 crows. And it has works and names by which it is invoked: "O Allah, indeed I ask You — O Grateful, O Appreciative, O Shīn, O Shayd, O Severe — by what You lodged in the letter Shīn of the hidden secrets and the stored lights — that You subject to me Your noble angels, the servants of this letter. Indeed You are over all things Able." ( The Letter Ṣād ) It is a speaking letter, dry, its nature earthy, its coldness exceeding its dryness. And whoever writes it upon gazelle-parchment 14 times on Friday with ink, and carries it and goes out to hunt — the wild beasts hasten to him, and the hunt is made easy for him. And whoever writes its squared form with its numbers falling upon it, upon a lead sheet, and on the other face a fish is depicted, and around it fourteen Indian Ṣād's, and hangs the sheet in a thread and lowers it at the river-bank — the fish hasten to him from every side until he can catch by hand around the sacrifice. And it has a strange talisman of great benefit — whoever writes it in a square ninety-five times, which is the number of its spelling, and draws a circle around the square, and writes around it from the outside 14 Arabic Ṣād's, and carries it with him — he is safe from thieves in travel and at home.
