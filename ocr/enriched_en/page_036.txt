الْوَقْتَ، وَمَحَا مِنْ نُورِهِ تِسْعَةً وَتِسْعِينَ جُزْءًا لِقَوْلِهِ تَعَالَى: فَمَحَوْنَا آيَةَ اللَّيْلِ وَجَعَلْنَا آيَةَ النَّهَارِ مُبْصِرَةً، وَلَوْلَا

عَلِيُّ بْنُ أَبِي طَالِبٍ كَرَّمَ اللَّهُ وَجْهَهُ: إِنَّ النَّوْمَ تَحْتَ الْقَمَرِ عُرْيَانًا يُورِثُ الْبَرَصَ، وَإِنَّ الثَّوْبَ إِذَا غُسِلَ
وَعُلِّقَ فِي الْقَمَرِ فَإِنَّهُ يَتَهَرَّى وَيَتَغَيَّرُ لَوْنُهُ، وَاللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ.

( فَصْلٌ: الْقَمَرُ يُؤَخَّرُ كُلَّ لَيْلَةٍ فِي مَنْزِلَةٍ مِنْهَا وَهَذِهِ أَسْمَاؤُهَا )
شَرَطَيْنِ، بَطَيْنِ، ثُرَيَّا، دَبَرَانِ، هَقْعَةٍ، هَنْعَةٍ، ذِرَاعٍ، نَثْرَةٍ، طَرْفَةٍ، جَبْهَةٍ، خُرْثَانِ، صَرْفَةٍ، عِوَا، سِمَاكٍ،
غَفْرٍ، زَبَانَا، إِكْلِيلٍ، قَلْبٍ، شَوْلَةٍ، نَعَائِمَ، بَلْدَةٍ، سُعُودٍ، سَعْدِ الذَّابِحِ، سَعْدِ بَلَعٍ، أَخْبِيَةٍ، مُقَدَّمٍ، مُؤَخَّرٍ، رِشَا.

( فَصْلٌ فِي النُّجُومِ الْأَرْبَعَةِ وَإِشَارَاتِهَا وَتَدْبِيرِهَا )
قَالَ تَعَالَى: وَهُوَ الَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمُ النُّجُومَ لِتَهْتَدُوا بِهَا فِي ظُلُمَاتِ الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ، وَقَالَ تَعَالَى: وَبِالنَّجْمِ هُمْ
يَهْتَدُونَ، وَرُوِيَ عَنْ سَعِيدِ بْنِ جُبَيْرٍ عَنِ ابْنِ عَبَّاسٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ أَنَّهُ قَالَ: عِلْمُ النُّجُومِ عَالٍ نَافِعٌ عَجَزَ عَنْهُ
كَثِيرٌ مِنَ النَّاسِ، وَأَشَارَ إِلَى مَعْرِفَةِ النُّجُومِ لِأَلَى الْأَحْكَامِ الَّتِي عَلَيْهَا الْحُرُمَاتُ، وَاتَّفَقُوا عَلَى أَنَّ نُورَ الْقَمَرِ
مِنْ نُورِ الشَّمْسِ، وَاخْتَلَفُوا فِي الْكَوَاكِبِ الْمَعْرُوفَةِ وَهِيَ اثْنَانِ وَعِشْرُونَ كَوْكَبًا، فَمِنْهَا الْجَدْيُ وَهُوَ مِنَ
الدَّوَالِ عَلَى الْقِبْلَةِ، وَالْجَدْيُ نَجْمٌ إِلَى جَانِبِ الْقُطْبِ الشَّمَالِيِّ حَوْلَهُ أَنْجُمٌ صِغَارٌ ثَلَاثٌ مِنْ فَوْقٍ وَ٣ مِنْ تَحْتَ،
أَحَدُهُمَا الْفَرْقَدَانِ وَفِي الْآخَرِ نَجْمٌ يُضِيءُ يُقَابِلُهَا، وَبَيْنَهَا وَبَيْنَ ذَلِكَ أَنْجُمٌ صِغَارٌ ٣ مِنْ فَوْقٍ وَ٣ مِنْ تَحْتَ
تَدُورُ حَوْلَ الْقُطْبِ، وَالْجَدْيُ لَا تَبْرَحُ مِنْ مَكَانِهِمَا، وَإِنَّمَا يُسْتَدَلُّ بِالْجَدْيِ عَلَى الْقُطْبِ، وَالْجَدْيُ قُطْبُ هَذِهِ
الْفِرَاشَةِ، وَقِيلَ الْقُطْبُ قُطْبُهَا، وَيُسْتَدَلُّ عَلَيْهَا بِالْجَدْيِ إِذَا لَمْ يَكُنْ قَمَرٌ، فَإِذَا قَوِيَ الْقَمَرُ فَلَا يَرَاهُ إِلَّا حَدِيدُ
الْبَصَرِ، وَالسُّهَا إِلَى جَانِبِهِ وَهُوَ نَجْمٌ خَفِيٌّ يُمْتَحَنُ بِهِ النَّاسُ أَبْصَارَهُمْ، وَالْجَدْيُ الَّذِي يُعْرَفُ بِهِ الْقِبْلَةُ وَهِيَ
جَدْيُ بَنَاتِ نَعْشٍ، وَثَلَاثُ بَنَاتٍ فَنَّ الْأَرْبَعَةِ وَهَا الْمُتَقَدِّمَانِ، وَمِنَ الْبَنَاتِ الْجَدْيُ وَهُوَ
آخِرُهَا، وَالسُّهَا وَهُوَ كَوْكَبٌ خَفِيٌّ فِي بَنَاتِ نَعْشٍ، وَمِنْهُ الْمَثَلُ: أَرِهَا السُّهَى وَتَرَى الْقَمَرَ، وَكَيْفِيَّةُ مَعْرِفَةِ
الْقِبْلَةِ أَنْ تَجْعَلَهُ وَرَاءَ ظَهْرِكَ بِأَرْضِ الشَّامِ، وَفِي أَرْضِ الْعِرَاقِ مُقَابِلًا ظَهْرَ أُذُنِكَ الْيُمْنَى، وَفِي
مِصْرَ خَلْفَ الْيُسْرَى عَلَى عُلُوهَا يَكُونُ مُسْتَقْبِلًا بَابَ الْبَيْتِ إِلَى الْمَقَامِ، وَمَتَى اسْتَدْبَرْتَ الْفَرْقَدَيْنِ
أَوْ بَنَاتِ نَعْشٍ كُنْتَ مُسْتَقْبِلًا جِهَةَ الْكَعْبَةِ، وَأَمَّا الْفَرْقَدَانِ مُضِيئَانِ قَرِيبَانِ مِنَ الْقُطْبِ،
وَبَنَاتِ نَعْشٍ سَبْعَةُ كَوَاكِبَ، أَرْبَعَةٌ مِنْهَا نَعْشٌ وَثَلَاثٌ بَنَاتٌ، كَذَلِكَ بَنَاتُ نَعْشٍ الصُّغْرَى، وَالْقُطْبُ
الشَّمَالِيُّ وَالْجَنُوبِيُّ لَا يَبْلُغُهُمَا شَمْسٌ وَلَا قَمَرٌ، وَالْقُطْبُ الْجَنُوبِيُّ جَدْيُهُ يَطْلُعُ سُهَيْلٌ لَا يَظْهَرُ إِلَّا فِي جَزِيرَةِ
الْعَرَبِ، وَمِنْهَا سُهَيْلٌ إِلَى جَانِبِ الْقُطْبِ الْجَنُوبِيِّ، وَمَطَالِعُهُ مِنْ مَهَبِّ الْجَنُوبِ ثُمَّ يَصِيرُ بِجِهَةِ الْغَرْبِ
فَيَصِيرُ فِي قِبْلَةِ الْمُصَلَّى وَهُنَا يَغِيبُ، وَإِنَّ سُهَيْلًا كَوْكَبٌ مُنْفَرِدٌ عَنِ الْكَوَاكِبِ الْيَمَانِيَّةِ، وَمَطْلَعُهُ
عَلَى يَسَارِ الْقِبْلَةِ، وَيُرَى فِي جَمِيعِ أَرْضِ الْعَرَبِ مِنْ عِرَاقٍ وَشَامٍ، وَلَا يُرَى فِي بِلَادِ أَرْمِينِيَّةٍ، وَمِنْ طُلُوعِهِ
الْحِجَازُ، وَرُؤْيَتُهُ بِالْعِرَاقِ بِضْعَ عَشْرَةَ لَيْلَةً، وَقَالَ بَعْضُهُمْ: إِنَّ سُهَيْلًا نَجْمُ الْعَرَبِ، تَقُولُ: إِذَا طَلَعَ سُهَيْلٌ

English:
— 20 —

[UNCLEAR: the source continues its discussion of sight, stating that the Creator, glorified and exalted is He, is beyond our existence or our ability to encompass Him.] The Moon has benefits. Among them is that it is a lamp for creation at night and a miracle for our Prophet, may God bless him and grant him peace, because of His saying, exalted is He: “The Hour has drawn near and the Moon has split.” He determined it in mansions so that we might know the time by them, and He erased ninety-nine parts from its light, because of His saying, exalted is He: “Then We erased the sign of the night and made the sign of the day visible.” Had it not been for that, people would spread out in their livelihoods by night and day, and no one would know night from day.

The Commander of the Believers, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib, may God honor his face, said: “Sleeping naked beneath the Moon causes leprosy. When a garment is washed and hung in the moonlight, it frays and its color changes.” God knows best.

(Section: the Moon is delayed each night in one of the mansions, and these are their names)

al-Sharṭayn, al-Buṭayn, al-Thurayyā, al-Dabarān, al-Ḥaqʿah, al-Hanʿah, al-Dhirāʿ, al-Nathrah, al-Ṭarfah, al-Jabhah, al-Khurthān, al-Ṣarfah, al-ʿAwāʾ, al-Simāk, al-Ghafr, al-Zubānā, al-Iklīl, al-Qalb, al-Shawlah, al-Naʿāʾim, al-Baldah, al-Suʿūd, Saʿd al-Dhābiḥ, Saʿd Bulaʿ, al-Akhbiyah, al-Muqaddam, al-Muʾakhkhar, and al-Rashā.

(Section on the four stars, their indications, and their management)

The Exalted said: “It is He who made the stars for you, so that you may be guided by them in the darkness of the land and the sea”; and the Exalted said: “And by the star they are guided.” It was narrated from Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, from Ibn ʿAbbās, may God be pleased with him, that he said: “The knowledge of the stars is lofty and beneficial; many people have been incapable of it.” [UNCLEAR: the source then points to knowledge of the stars for rulings associated with sacred restrictions.]

They agreed that the light of the Moon comes from the light of the Sun, but differed concerning the known stars, which are twenty-two stars. Among them is al-Jady, one of the indications used to determine the qiblah. Al-Jady is a star beside the North Pole. Around it are six small stars, three above and three below. One pair is al-Farqadān, and among the other stars is a bright star facing them. Between them and that star are six small stars, three above and three below, which revolve around the pole. [UNCLEAR: the source’s grammar about al-Jady’s remaining fixed and the relation of these stars is difficult.] Al-Jady is used to identify the pole; al-Jady is the pole of this [UNCLEAR: constellation called al-Firāshah]. It is also said that the pole is its pole.

Al-Jady is used to identify it when there is no Moon. When the Moon is bright, [UNCLEAR: the source says that only a sharp-eyed person can see it]. Al-Suhā is beside it, a faint star by which people test their eyesight. The al-Jady used to identify the qiblah is the Jady of Banāt Naʿsh. [UNCLEAR: the source then refers to three Banāt making four, to two preceding stars, and places al-Jady among the Banāt as their last.]

Al-Suhā is a faint star among Banāt Naʿsh. Hence the proverb: “Show her al-Suhā and she will see the Moon.” The method of knowing the qiblah is to place it behind your back in the land of al-Shām; in Iraq, opposite the back of your right ear; and in Egypt, behind the left [ear], above it. [UNCLEAR: the source then says that one faces the House’s door toward the Station.] Whenever you turn your back toward al-Farqadān or Banāt Naʿsh, you face the direction of the Kaʿbah.

The al-Farqadān are two bright stars close to the pole. Banāt Naʿsh are seven stars: four of them are the bier, and three are the daughters. The same applies to the Lesser Banāt Naʿsh. The North and South Poles are not reached by the Sun or the Moon.

The South Pole has its [UNCLEAR: corresponding] Jady. Suhayl rises, but it appears only in the Arabian Peninsula. Suhayl is beside the South Pole; its rising is from the direction of the south wind, then it moves toward the west, enters the qiblah of the place of prayer, and sets there. Suhayl is a solitary star, separate from the Yemeni stars. Its rising is to the left of the qiblah. It is seen throughout the Arab lands, in Iraq and al-Shām, but it is not seen in the lands of Armenia. [UNCLEAR: the source says that al-Ḥijāz is associated with its rising.] Its sighting in Iraq lasts some ten-odd nights.

Some said: “Suhayl is the star of the Arabs.” They say: “When Suhayl rises, do not feel safe from the torrent.” Know that the stars number one thousand and twenty-two. [UNCLEAR: the source’s final phrase says something about a tenth of a star in twelve and is not clear.]
