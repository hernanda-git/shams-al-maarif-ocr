English:

"…[the rule of inference:] where the Ascendant is the beginning of the look and the locus of the connotation of the question and the key of the door of the lodgings of the concealed [matters] in the inference — and the thirteenth [house] is the peg of the peg [watad al-watad] and the house of the querent, the proof of the state of partnership and its witness; and the fourteenth is the house of the consequence; and the fifteenth is the house of the one asked about and the partner of the sought of the Ascendant; and the sixteenth is the Balance of the work and the locus of the look of the rule in the results of the inference — so look into the bringing-forth of the connotation and its rules, and the making-speak of the answer, and all that occurs to the mind of the affairs of this world — all of them are among the hidden and the concealed between the first figure of the geomancy and the thirteenth, and between the fourth figure and the fourteenth; then the look to the Balance, the fifteenth — for in it is the speaking of the asked question and the rule in it: if it is a fortunate figure, [there is] connection and union in all affairs and upon all states; and if inauspicious, [there is] separation in all affairs and upon all states — upon what some of the shaykhs — may Allah have mercy on him — mentioned of it. And the outcome of the Ascendant-and-Balance, which is the sixteenth figure, is the pivot of the rule — the consequence of the affair according to what its relatings require and its significations give — and Allah knows best. (The Twenty-Seventh Intent: on the specialities of the figures in geomancy) The sixteen figures of geomancy are described according to their differing specialities and the relations mentioned: femininity and embodiment in the two of them, and by fortune and inauspiciousness and mixture in them by the opposite — blood, fixity, inversion, entry and exit, irradiation and lighting, darkness and effacement, silence and speech, truth and falsehood, right and falsehood [haqq and bāṭil], connection and separation, strength and weakness, movement and rest, speed and slowness, existence and non-existence, progeny and barrenness, straightness and crookedness, height and lowness, excellence and badness, nobility and life — and other than that. And they have relations to the elements, natures, temperaments, directions, seasons, days, nights, times, zodiacal signs, planets, letters, numbers, places, states, colors, forms, members of man, and kinds of animals — and other than that which is important for verification, confirmation, and precision for the soundness of inference and the correctness of works. And we have attained to a sufficient collection of that in our treatise 'Ghāyat al-Waṣl fī Mufradāt Buyūt al-Raml' [The Utmost Connection in the Particulars of the Houses of Geomancy], and our second treatise 'Jāmi'at al-Shaml fī Qawā'id A'māl al-Raml' [The Gatherer of Comprehension in the Rules of the Works of Geomancy] — upon a precision and exposition that may be hard to find in any other collection — and God Most High is the Patron of success and the Guide to the straight path. (The Twenty-Eighth Intent: on the specialities of the rules of the figures) The figures, as they are, according to their succession in the houses and their alighting in the settling — among them is what is good in the generality of positions at times, and among them what is the opposite, and among them what is nothing, and among them what is good in specific positions, and among them what is the reverse of that, and among them what is bad in one state [and] good in another state. And according to the consideration of these specialities is the rule of the relatings: (The First Type) the figures of 'Aqlah and Ijtimā' are good wherever they fall, and the consequence of their appearance in them is good — as [with] the Balance, and the appearance of 'Aqlah at the end of the hand from two praiseworthy figures indicates hidden treasures; and other than this, there is nothing in them. (The Second Type) Ḥumrah and Qabḍ Khārij — their alighting is bad in every place they fall, according to the specialities of the asked affair. (The Third Type) the figure of Ṭarīq — there is nothing good in it, and al-Jawdah does not indicate good or evil; and al-Jamā'ah — there is no evil in it, though in it is heaviness.
