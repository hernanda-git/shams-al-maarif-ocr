(A benefit) It is related from one of the shaykhs, who said: If you have a need, wish for it to be fulfilled, and its fulfillment is slow in coming,
then go to one of the mosques, stand at its qiblah, turn toward God Most High, and say: "O God, toward You
I have directed myself, at Your door I have stood, in Your presence I have taken refuge, and to You I have put my request. Through Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him,
and his family, I have sought a means of approach to You; through Your friends and Your elect I have sought intercession. So pour forth, O God, my need [UNCLEAR: the source wording fa-afiḍ, 'pour forth,' is unusual here; its intended sense is uncertain], and relieve my distress." Then name
your need and what you want. After that, pray two rakʿahs, reciting in each rakʿah the Opening of the Book, "Say: O
disbelievers," al-Ikhlāṣ, and the two refuge surahs. In the last prostration, while you are prostrating, say: "And Job, when he called upon his Lord: 'Distress has touched me,
and You are the most merciful of the merciful.' So We answered him and removed the distress that was upon him, and gave him his family and their like with them, as a mercy from Us
and a reminder to the worshippers." Then raise your head, recite the taḥiyyāt, and give the salām. When you have finished the prayer, rise and stand facing the qiblah
and say: "O God, Your knowledge has made asking unnecessary for me. My God, the Arabs and the non-Arabs, when a seeker of protection seeks refuge with him, he grants him protection [UNCLEAR: the singular 'him/he' following these plural groups has an uncertain referent]; and You are the God
of the Arabs and the non-Arabs. I have taken refuge in You, so grant me protection, and do not send me back disappointed. I have hoped for an answer from You, so answer me and fulfill
my need, and grant me my wish; and what I seek is Your mercy, O most merciful of the merciful." Then ask God for your need, for God answers you
by His favor.
