English:
— 467 —

(Section on His name the Exalted, al-Majīd — continued) Know that the meaning of al-Majīd is the noble — He whose Essence is majestic and whose favors are abundant, so He is noble in Essence when coupled with beautiful action, and that is called glory (majd), and He is al-Mājid; but one of the two [words] is more indicative of exaggeration, and it returns to the meaning of al-Jalīl and al-Karīm, and we have presented their meanings. And the drawing near to Allah by His recitation night and day, and the addition to it of His name al-Bā'ith — and of its special properties is the elevation of rank among the creatures and attaining for the attraction of provision; and the addition to it of al-Razzāq; and that he wrote on a tablet with the name of the King and recited it with His name al-Jalīl — one removed from his post returned to it, and the wayfarer who recites it attains what he wills. And this is its figure. And its mention with the basmala: 'O Allah, You are al-Majīd, the Possessor of nobility, the Vast, the Beautiful, the Effuser upon the servants with glory and the increasing gifts. You coupled the nobility of Your Essence with the beauty of Your action and Your beautiful bounty in Your affection, by the station of Islam. And every peak of the highest assembly has glorified You. I ask You by the nobility of Your glory, O Mājid, for the people of glory; by the loftiness of His majesty, O Mājid, for the people of glory, by the uniqueness of Your ancient Word, the necessary, the One. I ask You that You regard me with the nobility of Your glorious glory and the precedence in Your kindness by Your beautiful actions, and make me, by the beauty of obedience and turning to You, glorified, with Your beloveds as a witness, by Your saints and Your messengers as a witness, and by the realization of Your Oneness, alone, O Allah, O Majīd. I ask You that You subordinate to me the servant of this name, Your servant RaṬĀ'ĪL — indeed You are over all things powerful.' (Section on His name the Exalted, al-Bā'ith) Know that al-Bā'ith is the Reliever of the distressed and the Answerer of the supplications of the askers — and none is that but Allah. And the callers to Allah are three: a caller whose supplication is coupled with constraint, so his supplication is answered; a second who calls with the tongue of his speech and his distress did not intensify, so that is coupled with sincerity, and he is provided patience over the difficulties; and the third is he whose poverty intensified and found no god, so Allah relieves him by the supplication. A fourth category: he who asks Allah to increase upon him of this world and expand his provision and his term — so that one is deluded, for he occupied his time with something unfitting for the supplication to Allah; and better than his supplication is that Allah bless him in his provision and grant him success for righteous work and the relief of the believers. As related from 'Umar al-Khurāsānī: he said 'I performed pilgrimage in a year, and when I was in some place I fell into a well on the road to Yathrib, so I said in myself "I will not seek relief except from Allah the Exalted." Then a group passed by me and I wanted to ask them for help, but the thought turned me back; and behold, a group came and said "We will stop up the mouth of the well lest anyone fall into it," so they stopped it over me with a great rock, and I did not seek relief for a little; and behold, a beast had dug the mouth of the well and removed the rock and brought me near, so he took me out and I calmed him and he carried me off, and it left me; and a crier called me: "O 'Umar, you sought relief from us, so we relieved you with what you did not reckon of deliverance." And know that the wayfarer, by watching the times and paucity of objection and clinging to the law, from the nearings that connect to Allah the Exalted — until he is bound to contentment with the decree and the measure at the first blow; and how excellent is the saying of Allah the Exalted regarding His prophet Mūsā (peace be upon him): "So he found Him at the right hand by the shore"; and how excellent is the saying of some of them: 'My beloveds cast me to conceal the love * so the understanding enriched me from You apart from the unveiling. You were gentle in my affair so I established my witness * overwhelm me, my God, with care and gentleness. You showed me in the unseen until as if * it points to the unseen that You are in the palm. You veiled Yourself from me, I found naught but loneliness * so befriend me with Your gentleness and Your affection. And You give life to a living-one — You are in love its very death-call * and this is a wonder: the existence of life with death.' And… [page ends]
