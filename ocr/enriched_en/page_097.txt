English:

In the keeper of the Name whose mention is exalted [UNCLEAR: the opening construction is incomplete] * through it you are protected from all hateful things and al-ghalt [UNCLEAR: the meaning of this final word is uncertain].
And invoke blessings morning and evening * upon the family and the companions, min dhullihim ḥūt [UNCLEAR: approximately "from their abasement, a fish"; the reading and connection of these closing words are uncertain].
I have sought a means of approach to You, my Lord, through their standing * and Your Most Beautiful Names when they are gathered together.
Know that I did not mention the name of the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, for several reasons. Know that, even if the Messenger of God were mentioned in this place, the light of these noble, blessed Names would be folded away. Through him, a means of approach to God Most High is sought after the supplication; the text states that the supplicant's need is then fulfilled.
As for what it contains from the Torah, there are six letters, namely [U+06DE] |||; what it contains from the Gospel is ‡ mīm; and what it contains from the Magnificent Qur'an is [U+06E9] |||. [UNCLEAR: these bracketed U+ tokens reversibly identify the source's signs, not decoded meanings or inferred geometry; the statement "six letters" is retained without reconciling it with the signs shown.] So understand this and conceal it. Here I mention to you, of its special properties, what can be mentioned and what I have been commanded to disclose, among the obscure secrets before which the knowers stopped short and at which those firmly rooted in knowledge observed proper conduct. As it is related concerning them that they say: "We believe in it; all is from our Lord, and none remembers except those possessed of understanding." And as it is related concerning them—the noble angels [UNCLEAR: the grammatical relationship between "related concerning them" and "the noble angels" is uncertain]—despite their knowledge of the heavenly and earthly dominion, when they said: "Glory be to You; we have no knowledge except what You have taught us. Indeed, You are the All-Knowing, the Wise."

( Section ) As for his saying—may God be pleased with him—"and a seal after three, muʿajjal [UNCLEAR: 'hastened' or 'hastening'; its grammatical connection is uncertain]; by every affliction inside the body, aqsamt [UNCLEAR: 'I/you swore'; the person and the connection of 'by' are uncertain]." The text explains: if a person is afflicted with an internal illness such as colic, weakness of the liver, pain in the heart, or something similar, and it has baffled the physicians, write for him the three rods and after them the seal, in this form, without a sinām ('hump'): ||| [U+06DE], repeated 7 times. [UNCLEAR: the U+ token identifies the source's sign, not its meaning or geometry.] Write it in a glass vessel; it is exposed to the stars for 3 nights and given to the patient whom we have mentioned to drink; the text claims that he recovers.

( Section ) As for his saying, "muʿajjal, all the kinds of torment in their entirety [UNCLEAR: muʿajjal may mean 'hastened' or 'hastening'; its relation to the following words is uncertain]." The text explains: if someone wrongs you and you cannot obtain redress from him, write the seal, the three rods, and the sinām ('hump'). This means: set up an effigy and join the letters to it in the name of the person you intend and his mother's name. Draw the seal, the three rods, and the sinān ('spear-point') on every limb of that effigy. You are to have placed that effigy on a board, that is, a plank of wood from a coffin for the dead; nail it onto that board and bury it near a vessel containing vinegar and ṭūbal [UNCLEAR: approximate reading; the substance intended is uncertain]. The text claims that the person for whom this working is done suffers great distress, and whenever that effigy melts, his body wastes away.

( Section ) As for his saying, "and a mīm for the flow of the blood of every person who has transgressed." The text explains: draw a figure on an unfired potsherd, and write on it the name of the person you intend and his mother's name, with the ink of grief, on the day of the conjunction of two luminaries, wa-hā, in the degree, before separation [UNCLEAR: the printed wa-hā and its link to 'in the degree' are unclear; the astronomical timing is garbled and has not been completed]. Write the mīm and after it the three rods together with the sinān ('spear-point') and the seal, inverted [UNCLEAR: whether 'inverted' applies only to the seal or to a wider sequence is uncertain]. It is then thrown into turbid water or into a deep, disused well. The text claims that at that very hour the need is fulfilled, and the person for whom the working is done does not cease bleeding from his bodily openings until he perishes.

( Section ) As for his saying, "a ladder by which you ascend the steps of loftiness." The text explains: write a ladder on the nail of your right thumb, and enter before the tyrants in litigation at the time of need, in all circumstances. According to the text's claim, you will be victorious over your adversaries in all circumstances, and you will be honored, obeyed, accepted in your actions, effective in your word, and brought close to their thoughts; they will fulfill all your needs. The text further directs: write on waraq khuṭṭāf [UNCLEAR: waraq means 'paper/leaves'; khuṭṭāf may denote a swallow or a hook; the intended material is uncertain], put it in red wax, and place it under the tongue. The text claims that you will not cease to be joyful and happy, elevated in standing wherever you alight; their tongues and the tongue of every possessor of evil will be bound for you, and no one's gaze will fall upon you without that person loving you and inclining toward you.
