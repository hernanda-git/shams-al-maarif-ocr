English:
— 7 —

The locus of Islam and the meanings of the letters there is difficult to explain; it is also the locus of the speaking faculty in the human being that governs the meanings of the will issuing from the soul. The second cavity is in the middle of the heart. It is the place of reflection and remembrance, a radiant light, the place of tranquility, and the place of the imagination in what the spirit casts into it. The third cavity is at its far end; it is the most delicate and subtle of them. It is called the fuʾād, and it is the place of faith, intellect, light, agency, secrets, the balance of the intellect, the subtleties of wisdom, and the life of nature through subtle heat. Therefore, this fuʾād has a luminous eye through which it perceives the realities of the malakūt, the secrets of the higher worlds, particulars and universals, and the measures of realities. It is the place of gifted lights and the secrets of the higher worlds. That is the insight by which it sees, concerning which God says: “It is not the eyes that are blind, but the hearts within the chests.”
The middle cavity, which is the place of passionate love, has a luminous eye through which the object of seeking is perceived. From it arise earnestness in seeking and longing for the sought thing. Because of its subtlety, it attaches more quickly to persons. Through it the world of visible dominion and all that it contains of God's creation is unveiled, and through it appreciation of beautiful things takes place. Then the first cavity has a luminous eye through which one looks upon the secrets of sensory things, the states of composite beings, and the realities of letters and their secrets, as well as the immense things God has deposited in them: the secrets of the Divine Names and the realities of their meanings. Through it, this becomes a gift to God's servants and a means of glorifying God by knowing Him through it and through what He has bestowed upon it by unveiling the secrets of sensory things. These are all the insights of the hearts, although people differ in the ways matters differ.
It was mentioned earlier, in the discussion of the times of insights and the subtleties of inner secrets, that the spirits of revelation in the Book of God are three: the Spirit of the Trustworthy One, the Spirit of Holiness, and the Spirit of the Command. The Spirit of the Trustworthy One indicates the first cavity because it is the intermediary between utterance and the tongue. It is the first rank of revelation in its descent, each according to what God has allotted to it of inspiration and revelation upon the hearts. After it comes the Spirit of Holiness, which consists of the lights of what reaches the second rank of the heart from the Preserved Tablet. Through it faith and intellectual insight are established, and the lights of wisdom, the lights of spiritual sources, and the subtleties of faith appear. Then there is the second rank, which is the locus of the Most Holy; it is also the locus of hearing and the locus of intellect. God says to His Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: “You cannot make the dead hear, nor can you make the deaf hear the call.” This does not refer to the death of the senses; rather, it refers to the death of unbelief and disobedience. Nor does deafness refer to the ears, because the faculty of hearing is present. Rather, it refers to the hearing within the fuʾād, which is the locus of intellect and the place where the Spirit of the Command descends, signifying empowerment and the reality of union. No one but Muḥammad was singled out for this descent, peace and blessings be upon him. We have explained the medicines of the hearts, their treasuries, their lights, and their insights in our book entitled Mawāqif al-Ghāyāt fī Asrār al-Riyāḍāt. Consult it; you will find it well-grounded, God willing. God says: “Indeed, the Most Merciful will appoint affection for those who believe and do righteous deeds.” That is, affection will be placed in their hearts, taking hold of their whole being, and they will love Him. This is because they cultivate their hearts with various forms of remembrance and Qurʾanic spiritual practice, leaving none of the works of the heart without strengthening themselves through it. They cultivate their souls only by cutting off habits and familiar customs, until affection from God is obtained for them. Then their speech becomes speech of wisdom and their movement an ascent through degrees. They cultivate their spirit with the realities of faith, the subtleties of Islam, the light of the secrets of the sacred law, and the religious lights, until the effects of affection appear upon the spirit. It then looks toward the return, unveiling what God has prepared of every kind of bliss for His friends and punishment for His enemies. Its nature increasingly seeks return to God and yearns for Him. Its intellect increases in reflection upon created things and upon the secrets of His signs that God has deposited in them, and it cultivates it by abandoning everything...
