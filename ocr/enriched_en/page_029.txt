English:
12

The Ḍād acts there for love, stirs desire, and causes souls to expand through affection. It is suitable for managing every quality, treating spiritual entities, and setting up talismans. In it, incomplete medicines are gathered. Enter upon kings and chiefs. Whoever is born in it will have a praiseworthy outcome. Its incense is male frankincense, black seed, and saffron. God knows best.

[Discourse on the mansion of al-Fargh al-Muʾakhkhar, and this is its form]

Its form is two dots (0 0), and its letter is ẓāʾ. When the Moon descends in it, it is a fortunate watery planet. In it, by the permission of God Most High, a spiritual being operates and manages unpraised actions, such as advancing through unlucky mansions. Avoid war, meeting enemies and adversaries, and shedding blood in it. It is suitable for phlebotomy, cupping, bloodletting procedures, and binding against intercourse. It is suitable for entering the bathhouse, removing hair and nails, and drinking beneficial medicines. Whoever is born in it will be wicked and treacherous. Its incense is pepper and Chinese cinnamon. God knows best.

[Discourse on the mansion of al-Rashā, and this is its form]

Its form is four dots (0 0 0 0), and its letter is ghayn. When the Moon descends in it, it is a watery planet. Through it, by the permission of God Most High, a spiritual being of praiseworthy outcome descends into the world. Work talismans and good works in it. The craft of the honored stone and the dark jewel is managed in it; treat spiritual entities in it, and all works in it are praiseworthy. It is suitable for travel, marriage, wearing new clothes, moving from one place to another, and associating with sages and chiefs. Whoever is born in it will be blessed. Its incense is black seed. God knows best.

[Chapter on knowing the origins of the mansions]

This is another account, different from the preceding one. You say that its beginning is al-Sharṭayn: two separate stars, one toward the south and the other toward the north. They are the two horns of Aries, and the brighter of them is called al-Nāṭiḥ. In visual observation, when they are midway across the sky, the distance between them is ten cubits. Near the two al-Simāk is a small star that sometimes precedes them, and this is its form: 0 0 0.

As for al-Buṭayn, they are three small stars, dim and strongly variable. It is the belly of Aries, and it is small only because there are many stars in the shape of Aries. Al-Buṭayn is its belly, al-Thurayyā its rump, and al-Sharṭayn its horns. The form of al-Buṭayn is as follows: 0 0.

As for al-Thurayyā, it is seven stars: six of them are visible, and one is small and hidden, by which people test their eyesight. It was named Thurayyā from al-tharwah, meaning an abundance of dew and drizzle. It has among its names al-najm, “the star”; although numerically it is several stars, some scholars say that what is meant by His saying, “By the star when it falls,” is al-Thurayyā, according to most opinions. The Arabs call al-Thurayyā “mā,” even though numerically it is a group of stars. The Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, referred to it as a star and said: “When the star rises, afflictions are lifted from fruits and other things.” By “the star” he meant al-Thurayyā.
