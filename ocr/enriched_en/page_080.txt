Among these are the verses of al-Laṭīf in the Qur'an, in four places. The first is in Sūrat al-Anʿām, the words of God Most High: 'Eyes do not perceive Him, but He perceives eyes, and He is the Gentle, the All-Aware.' This verse is beneficial for one who fears for himself an enemy, an oppressor, or a tyrant: let him invoke His name as His name al-Laṭīf, morning and evening, 129 times, reciting it after the verse just mentioned; he will see wonders of God's gentleness, and God will make him safe from his enemy. * The second is in Sūrat Yūsuf, the words of God Most High: 'Indeed my Lord is gentle in what He wills; indeed He is the All-Knowing, the Wise.' Its special property is for one who fears hardship, an ordeal, straitened circumstances, anguish, or grief: let him invoke His name Laṭīf according to its numerical count and recite the verse; God will deliver him from that and from every worry. * The third is in Sūrat al-Shūrā, the words of God Most High: 'God is gentle with His servants; He provides for whom He wills, and He is the Strong, the Mighty.' Its special property is for one who has become poor: he recites His name al-Laṭīf, recites this verse, and does not persist in it; worldly goods will come to him. * The fourth is in Sūrat al-Mulk, the words of God Most High: 'Does He who created not know, when He is the Gentle, the All-Aware?' Its special property is for one seeking appointment to an office, such as a judge, a governor, or the like: let him recite His name al-Laṭīf morning and evening and recite the verse; what is sought will be obtained. * Let us return to the benefits of al-Fātiḥah that we were discussing: if it is written and its writing washed off with rainwater, the sick person wipes his face and hands with it once, drinks that water 3 times, and, when drinking it, says, 'O God, heal; You are the Healer. Suffice; You are the One who suffices. Grant well-being; You are the One who grants well-being,' 3 times, God heals him of his illness immediately, unless his appointed time of death has already arrived. * If someone with palpitations in his heart or ḥayf [UNCLEAR: the meaning of ḥayf here is uncertain] drinks some of that water, that condition leaves him.
