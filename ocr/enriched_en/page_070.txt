Ibn al-Ḥanafiyyah was asked about kāf hāʾ yāʾ ʿayn ṣād, and said to the questioner: "If I told you its interpretation, you would walk upon water by it, and your feet would not become wet." (Section) Each of the fourteen letters at the beginnings of the surahs has a meaning and something which, if God Most High disclosed it to the servant, would bring him a karamah from His presence. It is established in the hadith from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that he said to his companions: "When you meet the enemy, invoke: 'ḥāʾ mīm; they shall not be helped.'" Ḥāʾ mīm is among the hidden inward names of God Most High, so know this. Sahl ibn ʿAbd Allāh al-Tustarī, may God Most High have mercy on him, said: The noblest of all the alphabetic letters are the nine letters; the alphabetic letters are clothed in their light. They are these: (alif, lām, rāʾ, ḥāʾ, mīm, kāf, lām, ṣād). The outward bodies indicate them and their nobility: these are the seven heavens, the Footstool, and the Throne. They are the seven embodied forms to which God Most High alluded in His saying, Most High: alif lām mīm, ṣād, kāf hāʾ yāʾ ʿayn ṣād, ṭāʾ sīn. These are the fourteen letters. It is said that they are the Greatest Name of God, outward and inward. What the shaykhs among the people of spiritual realization, the imams of religion, and the scholars of religious law and realization have indicated is that the Greatest Name of God is among the outward names; consensus almost formed on this. The interpretation of this Name is that it brings things out of nonexistence into existence. Its alif points to the noble Essence, and it has the letter ḥāʾ for the utterance of the secret; [UNCLEAR: "and it is from it, if"; the connection is broken] the breast is the secret of knowledge, in general and in detail. Through it is the favor bestowed upon the Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, in His saying: "Have We not expanded your breast for you and removed your burden from you?" For it expands breasts. Since the alif is too exalted to be described by motion and stillness, because of its separation in beginningless eternity, and to it the ends of all aims return, it is in the Hereafter with motion. Motion is bound to the four vocalizations: ḍamm, naṣb, jarr, and sukūn, [UNCLEAR: "is struck from definition"; the wording is broken], and it has no need of divine enabling.
