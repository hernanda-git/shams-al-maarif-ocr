Answer, O Yārdūqāʾīl! Its meaning is: I am God, the Manifest, the Hidden in everything; and it is written on the palm of Isrāfīl, peace be upon him, and its bearer and its reciter — God makes every difficulty easy for the bearer of it, and the earth is rolled up for that bearer. And when the bearer of the name seeks and questions his spirits concerning anything, they answer him about it with all the news from all the earth, from the cities and the villages.

* And whoever wants to see something in his dream: he writes the name on the thumb of his hand and places that hand under his own head, and sleeps after saying, 'Answer, O servant of this name, and tell me about such-and-such.' Then he reads the name until he falls asleep; and then it comes to him in his dream and tells him the matter as it is. And if he has not seen on the first night, he repeats the work — the first time and the second time.

* And some of them mention that a man rose to ask God Most High for a need, and the man persisted 30 years and did not tire; and when God knew the sincerity of his intention, He granted it to him. 'Bāghīj — answer, O Samsamāʾīl!' Its meaning in Arabic: I am He who beholds the deep. Whoever recites it over his crop, the crop is not spoiled, and the human being is safe from drowning. And it is written on the palm of Kasfiyāʾīl: 'Yā Malīṭā, Yā Ṭardiyāʾīl.' And by this Name God Most High returned to Solomon his kingdom and his ring: 'Ismaʿūnī, Yā Qullā.' 'Answer, O Ṭūṭiyāʾīl!' Its meaning: I am the Ever-Living, the Sublime, and they are ruins. And these names you see

* except for the whole of it, when its letters are written apart, it wards off foul winds, and toothache when it is written and carried; and if it is written on a morsel and the one in pain chews it, his pain is stilled at that moment; and if the practitioner makes a seal and seals it with it upon clay from sown land, and buries it in a crop, the locust shall not eat it and no thing shall befall it. 'Yā Saṭīḥ, Yā Baṭiyāʾīl, Yā Ṣaghīr — answer, and with it over it: Yīl, Yā Hū, O He of whom none knows what He is except Himself.' And this is the explanation of the first Name, whose meaning in Arabic is: I am God, the One, the Subduer. And by this Name God gave victory to the believers over the unbelievers and the hypocrites. 'Yā Samīʿnā, Yā Nūr, Yāʾīl, Yā ʿAlimnā' — its meaning in Arabic: I am the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing, who faces the sun from the east to the west. Whoever recites it on a palmful of dust and throws it at the faces of the enemies, they scatter, and he says 'Shāhat al-wujūh' three times, and then they

* are defeated. 'O He before whom perish the worlds and the dominion, and He alone remains. O He than whom there is no god but He, the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden' — whoever recites these, God saves him from every distress. 'Yā Saṭīḥ bi-l-kūshayyā — answer, O Ṣarfāʾīl!' Its meaning: I am the Able for every distress, and the One who descends the Scrolls and the Secrets upon the hearts of the prophets, the righteous, and the good. Whoever calls upon it, God grants him preservation in regard to all that he hears; and whoever carries it is accepted. 'Yā Lyawhab, Yāh, Wāh'

* and in a narration, 'Wāh, Yāh, Wāh' — and the interpretation is like the first, then Thimūd. 'Yā Ṣāliḥ, Hīlū, Ḥam — answer, O Khafiyāʾīl, Isʿafāʾīl!' Its meaning: I am God, Lord of the worlds, the King, the Great, the Exalted. And by this Name God created the Throne and the Footstool. Whoever has these Names with him is protected from the jinn, from humankind, and from the devils. 'Yā Shamkhīnā, Yā Rabb, Yīnj, Ḥanīthā' — its meaning: I am He who says to a thing, 'Be,' and it is. There is no power in any of the created things. Whoever has these Names with him is in God's safekeeping, and in God's deliverance from that which he dislikes.

* And whoever recites it over water and gives it to one who is afraid, his terror is stilled. 'Yā Hayṭalū, Yā Yārūd, Aṭlamā, Shūmā' — its meaning in Arabic: I am the Subduer of servants, and the One who holds them to account for what they used to do. So if it is written upon a stone that has been taken out of a furnace, and a palmful is cast of it, and the Names are drawn upon it with the point of an iron nail, and the stone is thrown between a people gathered together upon their sins, he says at the time of the throwing: 'And We have cast between them enmity and hatred until the Day of Resurrection. Whenever they kindle a fire for war, the devil sets it ablaze among them; that day they scatter — and indeed they scatter.' 'Bifrshīyā Rābiyā Yāhū.' 'Yā Mā' — its meaning in Arabic: I am He who conceals the wronged from the eyes of the wrongdoers. If it is written on sand and a person sits upon it and reads over it the word of God Most High, 'And We have set between them a barrier,' up to His word, 'so they do not see,' 'Shāhat al-wujūh,' and He says, 'Take away their eyes and their sight
and make them blind.'
