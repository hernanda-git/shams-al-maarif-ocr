. الْقِذَة

English:
— 34 —

In the name of God, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful. The cloud fled to the east, the winds became still, the seas surged, and they listened to it by God’s permission. The devils were pelted from heaven, and the Lord of Might swore: “My name is not pronounced over one sick with longing, nor over anything for which there is no escape.” [UNCLEAR: the source’s wording in this sentence is damaged.]

Ibn Masʿūd, may God Most High be pleased with him, said: “Whoever wants God to save him from the nineteen al-zāniyah [UNCLEAR: the source uses this unclear term, literally related to ‘adulteresses’], let him write it. It consists of nineteen letters; every letter is salvation from one of them. Whoever recites it frequently is granted awe in the upper and lower worlds. Through it the kingdom of Solomon son of David, peace be upon them both, was established. Whoever writes it one hundred times and carries it is granted awe in people’s hearts.”

It is narrated from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar, may God Most High be pleased with him, that he said: “Whoever has a need from God Most High should fast on Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday. When Friday comes, he should bathe, go to the congregational mosque, and give something in charity. After performing the Friday prayer, he should say: ‘O God, I ask You by Your name, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful: God, there is no deity except Him, the Living, the Sustainer, to the end of the verse—the One before whom faces bow, voices become humble, and hearts tremble out of fear of Him. I ask You to bless and grant peace to our master Muḥammad, his family, and his Companions, and to grant my need, which is such-and-such,’ naming the need.” He used to say: “Do not teach it to your fools, lest some of them pray against others and their prayers be answered immediately.”

It is narrated from the Prophet, may God bless him and grant him peace, that he said: “The distance between the basmalah, ‘In the name of God, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful,’ and the Greatest Name of God is only like the distance between the whiteness of the eye and its blackness.” The Prophet, may God bless him and grant him peace, also said: “There is nothing between human beings and the devils except the basmalah, ‘In the name of God, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.’ It is the concealed name indicating that what follows it is the Greatest Name, namely God, because the Greatest Name is the Name of Majesty. It is the pole of the names, and they return to it. Among the names it is like a proper name: if you ask by the Most Compassionate, you say ‘God,’ and likewise the other names are attached to it and known through its majesty and exalted elevation.

“It has an additional distinction over the other names: if you remove the letter alif from it, ‘lillah’ remains; if you remove the first lām, ‘lah’ remains; and if you remove the second as well, ‘huwa’ remains. Thus every letter of it stands by itself. This is not so with the other names, because if you remove a letter from them, their meaning is lost. [UNCLEAR: the source says that the letters of this Greatest Name have not been disrupted or altered.] This is a distinction over the names and proof that it is the honored Essence, firmly possessing might and permanence.

“It has another distinction indicating the unique lordly Essence and the oneness of divinity, and indicating the unity of worship. Its first letter is alif, the first of the letters and the first of the numbers; its last is the letter hāʾ, which points to the unity of godhead and is not found in the other names. By its very state it says: ‘I am the First, the Last, the Manifest, and the Hidden.’ Then He followed it with the attribute of mercifulness. The Most High said: ‘Call upon God or call upon the Most Compassionate; whichever you call upon, He gives you the choice between saying “O God” or “O Most Compassionate.”’

“For He combines the two merciful attributes and the noble speech. If you seek mercy, say ‘O Most Compassionate’; this is more specific, because God Most High is the most specific of the names and, by agreement, the greatest of them. It is a divine name. As for its explanation, it is that He brings things out of nonexistence into existence. It has other meanings as well. Whoever studies them must withhold them from fools, lest they use them to reach reprehensible and forbidden acts, and it should then fall before God like ‘bāʿ.’ [UNCLEAR: the source’s expression ‘like bāʿ’ and the following wording are obscure.] When God Most High intends disobedience through it [UNCLEAR: the source’s wording is unclear], we seek refuge in God Most High from His anger. O God, do not make us among those who use Your names to aid their disobedience.”

“This name has four letters—alif, lām, lām, and hāʾ [UNCLEAR: the source names alif, lām, and hāʾ while counting four]. It has four natures, four directions—east, west, north, and south—and four angels of glorification: Jibrīl, Mīkāʾīl, Isrāfīl, and ʿIzrāʾīl.

“Jibrīl bears the message to the messengers and is associated with victory and overpowering; through him God Most High destroyed the disbelievers among the nations.
