And it is of the excessive numbers whose parts 10 point to His name al-Munjī (the Saviour) for what the meaning of sufficiency requires of need for other than Him. "They reckoned with us and were precise * then favoured and freed [us] *

( The Forty-Second Chapter: On His name, exalted, { al-Jalīl } ) Whoever abounds in its remembrance is magnified in the insights of people and every one who sees him reveres him; and whoever draws it and carries it with him subdues by it every rebellious tyrant, and crushes what was absent as his deed — so what appeared. And Shaykh Zayn al-Dīn al-Kāfī said: "This name has a majestic secret for the seekers of awe and majesty; and whoever abounds in its remembrance, no one can look at him out of veneration, and no tyrant's look falls on him but is terrified at seeing him" — so the bitterness of majesty was upon him as long as he was looked at. [It has] the number 73, which is a primal number, for the meaning of al-Jalīl is, by its comprehensiveness and gentleness, a seamlessness with no splitting; and the Jīm appeared in it for indication of the gathering — therefore the names of its letters exceed their named [count] by this number, which is 341, pointing to His name, exalted, Ṣamad (the Eternal), to His name Mu'īd (the Restorer), and to His name al-Munjī by the Hā' — so al-Jalīl is He to whom one repairs in every matter to obtain every good and be saved from every evil.
