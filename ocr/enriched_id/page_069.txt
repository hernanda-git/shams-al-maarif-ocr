English:
— 63 —

The opening letters of the sūrahs are taken from the names of Allah, exalted. And Abū al-'Āliyah said: "There is not among them a letter but it is a key to a name among the names of Allah: so the alif is from Allah, the lām from Latīf, the mīm from Mālik, the ṣād from Ṣādiq, the rā' from Rabb, the kāf from Karīm, the ṭā' from Ṭayyib, the sīn from Samī', the ḥā' from Ḥamīd, the qāf from Qadīr, the nūn from Nūr."

And this is their description as Abū al-'Āliyah, may Allah have mercy on him, arranged them: Alif-Lām-Mīm; Alif-Lām-Mīm-Ṣād; Alif-Lām-Mīm-Rā'; Kāf-Hā'-Yā'-'Ayn-Ṣād; Ṭā'-Hā'; Ṭā'-Sīn; Ṭā'-Sīn-Mīm; Yā'-Sīn; Ḥā'-Mīm; Ḥā'-Mīm-'Ayn-Sīn-Qāf; Qāf; Nūn. So he made the middle letter a letter of indication—which are the hā' and the bā'—and placed first the letters of Alif-Lām-Mīm-Ṣād and Alif-Lām-Mīm-Rā' and Kāf-Hā'-Yā'-'Ayn-Ṣād and Ṭā'-Sīn and the ḥā' from Ḥā'-Mīm and the qāf from Qāf and the Noble Qur'ān and the nūn from Nūn and the Pen.

And Ibn 'Abbās, may Allah be pleased with them both, said regarding the meaning of Alif-Lām-Mīm: its meaning is "I am Allah"; and Alif-Lām-Mīm-Rā': "I am Allah, the Knowing, the Seeing"—so the alif conveys "I," the lām conveys "the name of Allah," the mīm conveys "every knowledge," the rā' conveys "the vision." And their order: Alif-Lām-Mīm; Alif-Lām-Mīm-Ṣād; Alif-Lām-Mīm-Rā'; Kāf-Hā'-Yā'-'Ayn-Ṣād; Ṭā'-Hā'; Ṭā'-Sīn; Ṭā'-Sīn-Mīm; Yā'-Sīn; Ḥā'-Mīm; Ḥā'-Mīm-'Ayn-Sīn-Qāf; Qāf; Nūn. And fourteen of the repeated ones fell away, among them Alif-Lām-Mīm and the Ḥawāmīm, for their letters are established in those fourteen sūrahs which were mentioned earlier.

And Abū al-'Āliyah pointed to the saying of Ibn 'Abbās, may Allah be pleased with them both, that the opening letters are the Greatest Name of Allah, and Allah knows best.

( The benefit of the names ) They are the number of the degrees of Paradise; from them knowledge separated and to them it returns, and from them the existents appeared. And the existents are a sign indicating the Beautiful Names. And the names have coursed through the conduct of spirits and bodies, and have taken the place of security from creation; so there is no existent but the names of Allah surround it with seeing and hearing. And the import of the name of divinity comprehends the meanings of all the names; so the alif is a standing letter from which the letters arose and from which they arise, and it is their essence. Its likes are the intellect, knowledge, the Throne, and the Preserved Tablet. And the three lāms—it is the connecting letter between the lower and the higher; its like is the Tablet, the Chair (Kursī), and the soul. After the lām is the mīm, which is a letter denoting completeness; its like is the body. So the alif is the first created, and the body is only for created things; and the rest of the meanings of the letters enter into the alif, and the alif is the structure of gathering and comprehensiveness, just as the letters are comprehended in knowledge. So understand the meaning of completion and interpenetration, and there will appear to you meanings of mighty spiritual secrets that connect by right guidance in their sciences; so understand that.

And know that the awliyā', may Allah be pleased with them, spoke in the science of letters and names upon radiant rarities, and there was poured upon them from the source of exclusivity upon the attainment of certainty in their hearts and sincerity; so they were distinguished in the science of names over others by three things: the first, that they understood the meanings of the ninety-nine names by divine aid and inspiration such as none other knows by consideration and proof. The second, that they knew hidden names beyond these ninety-nine. The third, that they were distinguished by access to the Greatest Name. As for the prophets, peace be upon them, they knew the ninety-nine by the light of revelation which the awliyā' did not know, for they knew the sciences of the hidden names from the science of the Greatest Name of Allah; and no name among these names is known as it truly is except by him who is named by it and described by its meaning—and He is Allah alone, with no partner. And beyond all these names which Allah taught His prophets and awliyā' is what Allah, exalted, exclusively reserved in the knowledge of the unseen with Him, and no sent prophet nor brought-near angel was made privy to it.

And the first thing with which Allah singled out the servant when He willed to take him as His friend was to teach him the knowledge by which he becomes a knowing friend (walī), and to single him out from the knowledge of the ninety-nine names, opening to him from them sciences which are not opened to the scholar by the path of consideration; then He raises him to the knowledge of the hidden and manifest names among them, just as the manifest returned to Allah, exalted. And after his knowledge, He teaches him the hidden things which are isolated letters—and they are the fourteen letters occurring in the Mighty Qur'ān.
