English:
— 20 —

The contemplation is because the Creator, glory be to Him and exalted, is beyond our existence, and in the moon there are benefits, among them that it is a lamp for creation by night, and a miracle for our Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, by His saying, exalted: "The Hour has come near, and the moon has split"; and He determined it as mansions so that we might know the time by it; and He erased from its light ninety-nine parts by His saying, exalted: "And We erased the sign of the night and made the sign of the day visible." And were it not for that, people would spread out in their livelihoods by night and day, and no one would know night from day. And the Commander of the Believers, 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, may Allah honour his face, said: "Sleeping naked beneath the moon causes leprosy; and when a garment is washed and hung in the moon, it wears out and its colour changes," and Allah knows best.

( Section: the moon is delayed each night in a mansion, and these are their names ) al-Sharṭayn, al-Buṭayn, al-Thurayyā, al-Dabarān, al-Haq'ah, al-Han'ah, al-Dhirā', al-Nathrah, al-Ṭarf, al-Jabhah, al-Khurthān, al-Ṣarfah, al-'Iwā', Simāk, al-Ghafr, al-Zabānā, al-Iklīl, al-Qalb, al-Shawlah, al-Na'ā'im, al-Baldah, al-Su'ūd, Sa'd al-Dhābih, Sa'd Bula', al-Akhbiyah, al-Muqaddam, al-Mu'akhkhar, Ri'shā.

( Section on the four stars and their indications and management ) He, exalted, said: "And it is He who placed for you the stars that you may be guided by them in the darkness of the land and the sea," and He, exalted, said: "And by the star they are guided." And it is narrated from Sa'īd ibn Jubayr from Ibn 'Abbās, may Allah be pleased with him, that he said: "The science of the stars is lofty and beneficial; many people fell short of it." And he pointed to the knowledge of the stars for the rulings upon which there are sanctities. And they agreed that the light of the moon is from the light of the sun, and they differed concerning the known stars, which are twenty-two stars. Among them is al-Jady, which is one of the indicators (dalā'il) towards the qiblah; and al-Jady is a star beside the north pole, around which are small stars—three above and three below—two of them being al-Farqadān, and in the other a shining star facing them, and between it and that are small stars, three above and three below, revolving around the pole; and al-Jady does not depart from its place. Rather, al-Jady is used to indicate the pole; al-Jady is the pole of this constellation (al-Firāshah), and it is said the pole is its pole, and it is indicated by al-Jady when there is no moon; and when the moon is strong, only one of keen sight sees it. And al-Suhā is beside it, a hidden star by which people's sight is tested; and al-Jady by which the qiblah is known is Banāt Na'sh; and the three Banāt, then the four (of al-Na'sh) and the two preceding, and from the Banāt is al-Jady, which is the last of them; and al-Suhā is a hidden star in Banāt Na'sh, from which the proverb: "Show her al-Suhā and she sees the moon." And the manner of knowing the qiblah: place it behind your back in the land of Shām; in the land of 'Irāq, opposite the right ear; and in Miṣr, behind the left (ear), and upon its elevation one faces the door of the House towards the Station. And whenever you turn your back on al-Farqadān or Banāt Na'sh, you are facing the direction of the Ka'bah. And al-Farqadān are two shining (stars) near the pole, and Banāt Na'sh are seven stars—four of them are the bier (Na'sh) and three are the Banāt; likewise the Lesser Banāt Na'sh. And the north and south poles are not reached by sun or moon. And the south pole's al-Jady—Suhayl rises, which does not appear except in the Arabian Peninsula; and from it is Suhayl, beside the south pole, its risings being from the south wind, then it comes to the west, thus it is in the qiblah of the oratory, and here it sets. And Suhayl is a star separate from the Yemeni stars; its rising is on the left of the qiblah, and it is seen in all the land of the Arabs—'Irāq and Shām—but is not seen in the lands of Armenia; and from its rising is al-Ḥijāz, and its sighting in 'Irāq is some ten-odd nights. And some said: "Suhayl is the star of the Arabs," they say: "When Suhayl rises, do not feel safe from the torrent." So know that the stars are one thousand and twenty-two stars, and that it is a tenth of a star in twelve.
