And they will open it without doubt * and how many a caller cries out in supplication.
And Mahmūd will appear after this * and will rule Syria without battle.
To him the fortresses of Syria will all submit * and he spends his wealth in every state.
And an army will appear from the lands of the Romans * toward Aleppo, as if its march were perfection.
In it are Rus, Burghulah, and Romans * and every distant one from the edge of the canal.
And they will descend from its western regions and it will be laid waste * the estates of Syria deserted and empty.
And Arabs and Turks will destroy their necks * seeking plunder after the battle.
And the army of the Arwām will return at 'asr * upon their heels, the clamor of gain.
So Shīraz will be inhabited, white and black * and a fortress with tall towers.
And no Islam will be in it after this * a station after the times of protraction.
And a day in Hamāh — what a day! * upon them will be from it calamity,
When they raise the building and construct it * and the smoke is lifted over the high ones.
The Compassionate will pour upon them a wind * and you will see it in the eyes and in the jars.
With us from it is a mighty day * in which the youths of men will be slain.
White [ships] like the boat, of planks * of Indian [make], firmly polished.
And as for the flood, it will appear soon * and will appear in Syria in an ugly state.
How many in the flood within the rank * and how many houses removing the families.
And three different banners * from Kalb, whose mines are the setting.
So Turk, Roman, and Egyptian * kings of the earth, breaking deeds.
Their meeting will be on Tuesday * at the dawn prayer, battle joined.
The snows of the Romans will appear from it * and the cross will be raised over the high ones.
A crier calls out with guarded speech * thus the devil in that discourse.
And they will retreat in an angry gathering * upon the Arwām with entreating speech.
And no return for the land of the Romans from them * save a lone man by guile.
And Turkīyā and Miṣriyyā altogether * and they will differ in say and tale.
The sword will remain in the Egyptian, killing * to the farthest hidden things like the jars.
And they will meet from Hamadhān a person * whose beauty was the light of the crescent.
Those are truly the signs of the Mahdī * who will rule the lands without fail.
And he will grasp the rod in his two palms * and the beasts of the mountains grow fond of him.
The lands and who is on them obey him * and unbelief and error are wiped from them.
And he brings the proofs which * creation receives in full.
